1565-1582\nOda vassal. Third son of Yoshinari; also Narutoshi or Nagasada. Nobunaga's page. Bright, quick-witted and handsome, he was loved by Nobunaga from boyhood. Much was hoped of him, but at Honnō-ji he fought for Nobunaga and died with him and his brothers.

1558-1584\nOda vassal. Second son of Yoshinari. After his father died he won merit under Nobunaga's heir Nobutada. Fighting the Nagashima Ikkō league and the Takeda, he was called "Demon Musashi". After Honnō-ji he fought for Hideyoshi and fell at Komaki-Nagakute.

1523-1570\nOda vassal. First served the Saitō. He won merit in the unification of Owari, at Okehazama and in many battles. As lord of Usayama Castle he guarded the south shore of Lake Biwa, but the Azai and Asakura attacked; he fought back but fell.

?-?\nEtchū clan chief. Son of Nagamoto. Inherited when his brother Nagazumi was banished. But his retainer Kojima Motoshige took real power, and the Jinbo became a puppet regime. It is said he gave the house back when Nagazumi returned to Etchū.

1562-1600\nToyotomi vassal. Son of Akamatsu Masahide. Fought at Shizugatake, Komaki-Nagakute, in Kyūshū and at Odawara. He studied Chinese learning under Fujiwara Seika. At Sekigahara he went over to the East, but for burning the town below Tottori Castle, Ieyasu had him kill himself.

1560-1615\nKuroda vassal, named Mototsugu. He served Kuroda Kanbee and Nagamasa, winning fame as a Samurai General: "Matabee of the Spear". Suspected of treason, he became a rōnin. Later, invited by Toyotomi Hideyori, he entered Ōsaka Castle and died fighting the Tokugawa.

1546-1604\nToyotomi vassal, styled Josui as a lay monk. Once a Kodera elder, he aided Hideyoshi's western campaign and then served as his strategist. His brilliant plans did much to win Hideyoshi the realm, but feared for his talent, he was kept at a distance in later years.
